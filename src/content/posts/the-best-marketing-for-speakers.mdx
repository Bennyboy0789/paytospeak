***What is the best marketing for speakers?***

***And "how" should you market yourself?***

I get these questions a lot from newer speakers and folks just starting out. I get them even more from speakers with some experience but who are struggling a bit.

They think, or assume, that speakers have to spend a lot of $$$ on expensive marketing, social media ads, website, demo videos, contact lists, databases and more.

**NOPE. Not me.** This approach (i.e. spending lots of $$ on marketing) hasn't worked for me, been there, done that AND already bought the t-shirt several times.

This approach also hasn't worked for numerous other full-time professional speakers I connect with regularly.

Throwing money out "hoping" you get business isn't good speaking business strategy. That's called hope and it's no different than throwing spaghetti on the wall hoping something will stick.

Especially in the speaking industry... one reason being is that there are SO MANY "speakers" out there already paying lots of $$$ to promote themselves. And most of these speakers are paying LOTS of money for little ROI (Return On Investment).

To quote Marc Cuban from Shark Tank, the reason most marketing is expensive and doesn't work is because "marketing professionals have to spend a lot of money to prove their worth."

**So here's my solution for you... and it's what I believe is the best marketing for speakers. And it's also how speakers at any level should focus on to market and promote themselves.**

This solution will help you not only focus on what you need to do next but also help you not waste $$$.

Ready? Here it comes...

The best marketing for any speaker at any level is this ONE THING.... *to have an incredible speech.*

> Nothing else matters if your speech needs work. Websites do not matter. Neither do demo videos or lists or databases... or anything else for that matter.

Even a Speakers Agent or a bureau can't help you if you're speech just isn't as good as you think it is.

Here's why, their brand and reputation is attached to the speaker as well.

Remember the speech IS the product of any speaking business. And if the product needs work, i.e. the speech, the business will consistently struggle.

An incredible speech is not only referable, but it's the heart of any speaking business.

> Unless you're 100% confident that your speech is 10/10 and you've also gotten that feedback consistently from numerous sources (including me), then focusing on your speech is target to start.

If a speech isn't that good, or if it's just mediocre, it will never be "talked about" after the speaker is finished. Audience members remember great speeches delivered by a great messenger - the speaker.
