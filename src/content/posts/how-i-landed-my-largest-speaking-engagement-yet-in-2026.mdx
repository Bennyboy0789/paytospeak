## How I Landed My Largest Speaking Engagement Yet in 2026 (Part 1)

This week, I had the opportunity to keynote for an incredible company called Net32, an online marketplace serving dental practices across the country. They are a fully remote company and this was their annual meeting with all employees coming together.

Think of it like “Amazon for dentists”, faster, easier, and more efficient than traditional purchasing through reps.

But here’s the thing… I’m not writing this because of *who* I spoke for. I’m writing this because of *how* I got booked.

Because there are lessons here.

So much so that I’m turning this into a short series:

- **Part 1: How I landed the engagement**
- **Part 2: How I prepared**
- **Part 3: How I delivered (and what happened next)**

## A Quick Reality Check (From My 6-Year-Old)

Right before I spoke, I FaceTimed my daughter.

“Daddy, where are you?”

“I’m about to go speak for a company.”

She paused… then asked:

**“Are you nervous?”**

I said, “A little.”

Her response?

**“Why are you nervous if this is what you do all the time?”**

…Mic drop. Perspective shift.

Repetition is the mother of skill, and sometimes the reminder you need comes from a 6-year-old.

## So… How Did I Land This Engagement?

Here’s exactly what happened:

### 1. They Found Me

They submitted an inquiry through my website. [Visit here to see what that looks like.](https://kevincsnyder.com/hire-kevin/)

Lesson: Your website should be working *for you*.

### 2. I Responded FAST

I replied within a few hours.

They actually commented on this.

Translation: Most speakers are slow to respond. Opportunity: Be the one who isn’t.

### 3. I Asked to Confirm the Budget 'Range' they Indicated on the Online Inquiry

How did I know their budget? Because it's one of the questions on my online inquiry form. But sometimes, there budget is higher or lower (or a range) compared to what they indicated.

Not awkward at all. Not pushy. Just professional.

Most speakers avoid this. I don’t.

Clients have told me they *appreciate it* because it speeds up decision-making.

Lesson: Asking smart questions reduces friction, it doesn’t create it.

### 4. I Prepared Like Crazy for the First Call

Before our first meeting, I:

- Researched their company deeply
- Found internal insights (goldmine)
- Even scheduled a dentist appointment to understand their industry

Yes… seriously.

Lesson: Preparation = differentiation

### 5. I Prepared Even MORE for the CEO Call

I was one of four speakers selected to meet with the CEO.

So I went deeper:

- Researched him personally
- Found shared connections/interests
- Built rapport quickly

At one point he said:

> “If you prepare your keynote like you prepared for this call, you’re at the top of my list.”

That’s when I knew.

### 6. Then Came the Curveball: “Send a Proposal”

I hadn’t been using formal proposals much lately. Didn’t need to. Options are already listed in my Speaker Packet which you can download off my website here: [***www.KevinCSnyder.com***](http://www.KevinCSnyder.com)

But this time? I leaned in.

### 7. I Built a Custom Proposal (Game Changer)

I documented:

- Their goals
- Their challenges
- Their language

Then I created a **2-page tailored proposal integrating all our transcribed conversations, my research, notes, etc.** (with help from ChatGPT).

I even added: A “Beyond the Keynote” package with follow-up sessions

Their response:

> “Kevin, you put the time in on every level… you’re the best fit.”

## Final Thought (For Now)

I didn’t land this because I’m “better.”

I landed it because I was:

- Faster
- More prepared
- More intentional
- More aligned

## What’s Next…

And yes… I delivered the keynote this week.

Standing ovation. Immediate referral.

And this from their CEO:
